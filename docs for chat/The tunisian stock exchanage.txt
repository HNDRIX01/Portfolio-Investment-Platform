TUNISIAN STOCK EXCHANGE 
The Stock Exchange is the place where investors buy and sell capital equities or debt securities issued by companies, the 
State and local collectivities. As a market it ensures the liquidity of the securities held by the investors. This liquidity provides 
the issuers with funds necessary to increase their expansion by calling for public shareholding. For this purpose, the Stock 
Exchange constitutes one of the major sources of financing the economy.
Our mission:
The management of the Tunisian transferable securities market by:
The admission of new securities to the Stock Exchange official list.
The organization of exchanges and the quotation of securities under the best conditions of fairness, safety and transparency.
The dissemination of exchange information.
The promotion and the improvement of the exchange market.
Our ambitions:
To be the driving force behind changing attitudes towards financial matters in order to make Stock Exchange saving an integral 
part of the practices of savers and to ingrain Stock Exchange financing in the managerial culture of Tunisian companies.
To constitute an essential part of the financial economy of Tunisia.
To be the right way for Tunisian companies to achieve their ambitions for rapid growth and improvement to reduce the delays in 
their development and their internationalization.
To participate in the widest possible dissemination of the culture of transparency and good governance.
To participate in Tunisia’s regional outreach.
Our assets :
A favorable economic and social environment: a solidly based economy with promising prospects, sound macro-economic 
management, controlled inflation and the tranquility of social stability.
A modern legislative and regulatory framework in compliance with international standards.
Advanced technology of negotiation guaranteeing performance and safety; Dematerialization of securities;
A reliable settlement-delivery system meeting the international standards with settlement day+3.
A system which guarantees the settlement of transactions.
Professionalism, financial strength and integrity are required from the market operators to guarantee the interests of investors.
Our place:
The Stock Exchange is the heart of the financial market where:
Only approved brokerage firms conduct the negotiation of securities.
The Company of deposit, clearing and settlement of securities (Tunisian Central Depository) ensures payment in cash and the 
delivery of securities.
The Market Guarantee Fund intervenes between the brokers to guarantee the settlement of transactions.
The Financial Market Council, as a body controlling the market, oversees the protection of the investors and the good functioning 
of the Stock Exchange markets.
The stock exchange markets:
The Stock Exchange official list is reserved for the negotiation of securities which meet the admission requirements provided 
for in the Stock Exchange regulations. It includes the following markets:
The Principal Market reserved for the large and high performance companies.
The Alternative Market reserved for small and medium-sized companies which have promising prospects.
The Bond Market reserved for the negotiation of debt securities.
The OTC market is open to the securities of pubic shareholding companies not admitted to the Stock Exchange official list.

1. What is the BVMT and its purpose?
The Bourse des Valeurs Mobilières de Tunis (BVMT), or the Tunisian Stock Exchange, is the 
official financial market institution responsible for facilitating the trading of securities in Tunisia. Its 
primary purpose is to provide a regulated, transparent, and efficient platform for companies to raise 
capital through the issuance of stocks and bonds, while enabling investors to trade these securities. 
The BVMT ensures market integrity, protects investor interests, and contributes to Tunisia’s economic 
development by promoting investment and liquidity in the financial sector.
2. History and Background of the Tunisian Stock Market
The Tunisian stock market traces its origins to 1969, when the first stock exchange was established. 
However, modern reforms began in the early 1990s to align with international standards. The BVMT

was officially created in 1994 under Law 94-117, replacing the earlier structure. This reform aimed to 
liberalize the economy, attract foreign investment, and integrate Tunisia into global financial markets. 
Over time, the BVMT introduced electronic trading (1996), dematerialized securities (1999), and 
expanded its listings to include diverse sectors such as banking, manufacturing, and 
telecommunications.
3. Structure and Organization of the BVMT
The BVMT operates as a self-regulated organization under the supervision of Tunisia’s Financial 
Market Council (Conseil du Marché Financier, CMF). Key components include:
Board of Directors: Governs strategic decisions and ensures regulatory compliance.
Trading Platforms: Fully electronic system for order matching and execution.
Listings Department: Manages company admissions, disclosure requirements, and delistings.
Surveillance Unit: Monitors trading activities to prevent market abuse and ensure fairness.
Clearing and Settlement: Managed by Tunisie Clearing, which guarantees secure post-trade 
processes (e.g., T+3 settlement cycle).
Members include brokerage firms, institutional investors, and listed companies.
4. Key Market Indices
TUNINDEX: The benchmark index tracking the performance of the 30 most liquid and capitalized 
stocks on the BVMT. It reflects overall market trends.
TUNINDEX 20: Comprises the top 20 companies by liquidity and market capitalization.
TUNINDEX ESG: Focuses on companies with strong environmental, social, and governance 
practices.
ALTUNINDEX: Tracks small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) listed on the Alternative 
Market.
5. Trading Hours and Procedures
Trading Hours: Sessions run Sunday to Thursday (excluding public holidays).
Pre-opening: 9:15 AM – 9:30 AM (orders are collected but not executed).
Continuous Trading: 9:30 AM – 12:30 PM.
Procedures:
Orders (market, limit, or block trades) are placed via licensed brokers.
Trades are executed electronically, with real-time price dissemination.
Settlement occurs three business days post-trade (T+3) through Tunisie Clearing.
6. Types of Securities Traded
Equities: Common and preferred shares of publicly listed companies.
Bonds: Government bonds (e.g., Treasury bills), corporate bonds, and supranational debt 
instruments.
Investment Funds: Units of collective investment schemes (UCITS and others).
Derivatives: Limited offerings, including futures and options (less active compared to equities).
Alternative Market Securities: Shares of SMEs listed on the dedicated Alternative Market to 
support smaller enterprises.
